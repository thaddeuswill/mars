# External Review Pack: Science Teachers

**Owner:** Lead Instructional Designer · **Status:** Draft 1, 2026-11-26 · **Decision:** the owner seeks three external reviewers before Stage 3 (TEAM.md Decision Log, round 3): a planetary scientist, a climate modeler, and science teachers. **This pack is the teachers' part.** The SME builds the science pack (planetary scientist, climate modeler) in `docs/science/`; the two packs don't overlap. Coordination is by file: items that are science calls go to the SME's pack, items that are classroom calls are here.

**Who:** ideally one middle school (grades 6–8) and one high school (9–12) science teacher; Earth, environmental, or general science. Plus, for the transfer key only, a carbon-cycle scientist if the SME's pack can include one.
**Time:** about 2–3 hours of reading and marking.
**What we send:** this page, the listed sections as a PDF or plain-text extract, and a one-page project summary. No student data exists; nothing personal is shared.
**What we ask for:** a mark on each item (✓ fine / ~ change / ✗ wrong) and a short comment. Teachers judge **how students will read and answer**; science correctness is the SME pack's job, but please flag anything that looks wrong.

Source: `docs/learning/rubrics.md`, "Transfer task: Earth's carbon cycle" and "Transfer task answer key (SME draft)".

|---|---|
| A1 | Task: "Draw the feedback loops in Earth's carbon cycle. Label one reinforcing and one balancing loop." Can your students (MS / HS) do this after a unit, with no extra scaffold? If not, what scaffold (a word bank, a partly drawn diagram)? |

| A6 | The teacher uncertainty note ("score direction, not size"). Clear enough? |

For each forecast:

| # | Question for the reviewer |
|---|---|
| B1 | Does each **wrong choice read as plausible but wrong** to a 12–14-year-old (MS) and to a 15–18-year-old (HS)? Mark any distractor that's too obviously wrong, or that a reasonable student could defend as right |

## How we use the answers

Anything a teacher marks ✗ is fixed or answered before Stage 3. Answers are design input, not data about students.
