# Mars surface materials and a block set for them

**Owner:** Subject Matter Expert · **Written:** 2026-11-27 · **For:** Lead Artist (textures), Technical Lead (worldgen, loot), Game Designer (use of each block)
**Why:** the owner accepted the W1 terrain and wants more surface block types so the ground is more accurate to Mars. The make-it-work rule (`docs/design/world-and-blocks.md` 0.2) applies: an invented block or simple chemistry is fine if labelled; vanilla crafting stays.
**Reads with:** `mars-terrain-reference.md` (shapes and palette), `mars-materials-and-recipes.md` (recipes). W1 blocks already built: `basaltic_sand`, `hematite_ore`, `dark_sand_ore`, `ground_ice` (`pack/data/mars/loot_table/blocks/`).

**Marks.** **VERIFIED** = I opened the page on 2026-11-27 (curl, HTML stripped with `python3 -I`, never run) and the quoted line is on it. **NOT VERIFIED** = not seen on a page I opened; do not put it in a journal until sourced. **REAL** = the material exists on Mars. **SIMPLIFIED** = real material, game-sized (placement, abundance or use is a game choice). **INVENTED** = the block or its use is made up; the journal says "Game invention". **NEW** = a block we do not have yet (vanilla blocks and our W1 blocks are not marked NEW). Colours are my reading of NASA text, not measured: the Artist should check real photos (search terms in section 3). All yields and uses are invented game design.

## 1. Short answer

1. The W1 set (dust, dark sand, rock, ice, hematite ore) covers about half of what a lander sees. The big gaps are **crust and layered rock** (the ground under the dust), **pebbles**, **breccia**, **spherules**, **salts** and **frost**.
2. Reusing vanilla gives most of it: terracotta bands, sandstone variants, gravel, smooth basalt, calcite, tuff, blackstone. About **11 NEW cube blocks** fill the rest, all plain cubes (a texture, a loot table, a tag). Tier A (do first) is 6 blocks; Tier B is 5 more.
3. Real Mars ground is **brown, tan, grey and rust**. The two bright exceptions (white veins, white frost) are thin. See section 4.

## 4. What the player should NOT see

| Do not show | Why | Allowed exception |
|---|---|---|
| **Green** blocks or tints (grass, moss, olivine as green, copper green) | Earth olivine is green but Mars rocks read grey-brown in true colour; green is the Phase 3 reward and must stay meaningful | Biocrust and plants after Phase 3 seeding, in the sealed dome |
| **Liquid water**, lakes, rivers, rain, wet mud | Surface liquid water cannot last now: NASA Mars Facts: "the Martian atmosphere is too thin for liquid water to exist for long on the surface" **VERIFIED** | Water only as buckets, tanks, ice, and in the dome. Late Phase 3 meltwater if the Index says so |
| **Earth soil**: `dirt`, `grass_block`, `podzol`, `mud`, `clay` as terrain | Teaches "Mars is soil like ours". Mars regolith is rusty dust and sand with salts | Farmland made in the greenhouse after the wash step (game design) |
| **Obsidian-black, netherrack-red, magma glow, deepslate, crimson or warped colours** | They suggest hell or a fantasy world. Real volcanic rock on Mars is dark grey-brown basalt | Dark basalt and vesicular basalt are real and allowed; if a block is very dark, the tooltip says "volcanic rock" |
| **White snow blankets**, blue ice | Mars frost is a thin, seasonal film and ground ice is hidden under dust | Thin `co2_frost`, polar bands, ground ice in cuts |
| **Bright white or glassy "crystal" blocks** (quartz, amethyst, prismarine) | The game's "sand ore" is dark sand, not silica (world-and-blocks 0.1 item 1) | Gypsum veins and carbonate as thin, pale features |
| **Blue or purple tints** | The sky is butterscotch and dusty by day; a blue sunset tint is a sky matter, not a block matter | Blue-grey hint on fresh basalt is **NOT VERIFIED**; leave it out |
| **Trees, vines, flowers, animals, villagers on Mars** | No known life | Only inside the dome |
| **Rolling grassy hills, river valleys** | Terrain reference section 4 | Craters, dunes, mesas |
