## 0. Scale rules (read first)

1 block = 1 m. A player walks about 4 to 5 blocks per second, so a 500-block trip is about 2 minutes. Real Mars landforms span 10 m to 4000 km. We keep **small things at true size** and **compress large things** (GAME CHOICE, label "stylised" in the journal).

| Size on real Mars | Treatment | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Rocks, ripples, small craters (up to about 100 m) | True size, 1:1 | The player sees these at eye height; this is what Viking, Pathfinder and Phoenix photos show |
| Dunes (tens of m), crater to 1 km | True size where possible, else lightly squeezed | Fits in loaded chunks |
| Large craters (km), mesas, ridges | Compressed 5 to 20 times (GAME CHOICE) | Cannot walk across 20 km |
| Canyons, volcanoes, polar cap | Stand-ins only, "stylised" (already agreed in the design doc) | Valles Marineris is thousands of km long |

Minecraft limit to respect: world height is 384 blocks (-64 to 320). Keep the ground between about y 40 and y 120 on the plains so a 60-block crater or cliff fits. This is a Technical Lead number: **INVENTED**.

## 2. How many rocks? (derived from a verified formula)

Source: https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/20030111177 **VERIFIED**: "Fk(D) = k exp [-q(k) D]" with "q(k) = 1.79 + 0.152/k"; F is the cumulative fraction of ground area covered by rocks of diameter D **or larger**, and k is the total rock-covered fraction. The abstract does not state the unit of D; the published model uses meters (**NOT VERIFIED**, check before quoting). My calculation (python, D in metres):

| Site type | k | % of ground covered by rocks of at least 0.5 m | Area of rocks at least 1 m, per 100 x 100 blocks | At least 0.5 m rocks per 100 x 100 blocks (about) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Smooth northern plain (Phoenix-like) | 0.02 | 0.02% | almost none | about 2 |
| Typical lander plain | 0.05 | 0.45% | 4 m2 (about 4 blocks) | about 45 |
| Rocky flood plain (Pathfinder-like) | 0.19 | 5.2% | 142 m2 | about 520 |
| Extreme rocky | 0.40 | 13.5% | 457 m2 | about 1350 |

**Game use:** one rock block (stone-type, 1 m) per rock of 0.5 m or larger is a fair reading at 1 block = 1 m, so on the dome plain place about **2 to 45 rock blocks per 10,000 columns**, 10 to 100 times more on crater rims, ejecta and flood channels. Pebbles under 0.5 m: texture or `stone_button`-like decorations, INVENTED density. Note the numbers are low: **real Mars plains look empty at block scale**, so add pebbles, ripples and craters to avoid a bare slab. Clump rocks (ejecta, scarp feet) rather than spreading them evenly (real rocks cluster; **NOT VERIFIED** here).

## 4. What generic hills get wrong

| Earth-style default | What real Mars shows | Fix in noise |
|---|---|---|
| Rolling hills everywhere, 10 to 40 blocks (the current W1 text) | Wide plains are very flat; the Phoenix first images show "no large rocks or hills". Relief comes from **craters, dunes, ridges and scarps**, not random hills | Cut base amplitude to 3 to 6; put relief only in specific biomes |
| Smooth, soft, round hill tops | Sharp rims, steep fresh crater walls, cliff-and-bench mesas | Use ridged noise, terracing, steps |
| Valleys cut by rivers, branching | Dry. Old flood channels are wide and streamlined, rocky; canyons are straight and huge | Few branches; broad dry channels with boulders |
| Trees, grass, green and blue tints | Only dust, rock, sand, ice. No plants | Keep all surface tints ochre / brown / grey |
| Even sprinkle of small things | Clustered rocks; many tiny craters; rocks heavy on ejecta | Cluster, scale by crater distance |
| Rain-worn gullies | Wind-made ripples and dunes, impact gardening | Ripple noise, not gullies |
| Hills sloping every direction | Dunes have a gentle side and a steep side, all facing the same way | One global wind direction per planet (INVENTED, add to planet data) |
| Water-filled low points | Dry basins hold dust and dunes | No water at start; basins = dune fields |
| Everything the same size | Few huge, many small (craters, rocks) | Power-law sizes (section 1 row 3, 5) |
