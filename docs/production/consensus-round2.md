## 1. Goal priority tiers (LEARNING.md §1, "Goal priorities")

### 1.1 Frame

When scope, schedule, or a design change forces a choice, which learning goals get resources first? The tiers decide **resources**, not whether a goal exists: every goal stays in the game and on the post-test, and goal IDs stay stable. The tiers directly set the order of the cut list ([budget.md §4.3](budget.md)).

**Must weigh in:** Lead Instructional Designer (owner), Lead Game Designer (appeal; owns the mechanics that carry each goal), Producer (scope). Asked for a perspective statement: SME (NGSS strength criterion), Technical Lead and Lead Artist (cost of the carriers).

### 1.3 Common ground

- Every goal stays taught and on the post-test; tiers set resource order only.

- The forecast system is protected regardless of B3's tier.
- Deemphasizing a goal's learning support doesn't remove the mechanic that carries it.
- Tiers are flexible and revisited after the first playtests.

### 1.6 What would make it better defined

### 1.7 Link to iteration: broad first, then drill down (ACEDT)

The user's Chapter 9 pages (ACEDT, via the coordinator) say to analyze learning objectives broadly across the whole domain early, then drill down on a subset each iteration; and if scope forces the game to teach only a subset well, that is a decision made **with the customer and the full team**. For us:
- The broad analysis is done (LEARNING.md §0–1). The tiers set the **drill-down order**: each iteration (ROADMAP "How We Iterate") works its phase's Primary goals first, then Secondary.
- The tiers are **not** permission for any one role to drop a goal. Taking any cut that reduces a goal's teaching, feedback, or measure below its tier's minimum goes to the user with a full-team perspective round, using this brief's options and the cut list in [budget.md](budget.md).
- This strengthens option T-C: the quadrant rule decides feature order, and the subset decision itself stays with the user.

## 2. Interruption rules (TEAM.md 8.6 vs. LEARNING.md §4.6)

### 2.1 Frame

When may feedback interrupt play? Interrupting at the wrong moment breaks flow immersion and frustrates (appeal); never interrupting lets reflection and forecasts go missing (measurement). The rules must be specific enough to be config values and testable in the Stage 3 playtest (TEAM.md 8.6 says so).

**Must weigh in:** Lead Instructional Designer and Lead Game Designer (joint owners), Producer. Asked for a perspective statement: Technical Lead (cost of the queue, rate caps, end-of-session signal, logging), Lead Artist (salience of log lines and chimes; never sound over voice), SME (expected: nothing to add; say so explicitly).

### 2.2 Positions, rule by rule

"Pop-up" = takes over the screen or needs a click. "Log line" = one line in the colony log or HUD, doesn't stop play. "Natural pause" = entering the dome, sleeping, opening the Monitor or journal, reaching a gate.

| # | TEAM.md 8.6 draft (coordinator) | Lead Instructional Designer (LEARNING.md §4.6) | Lead Game Designer | Producer view |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Never interrupt during danger, combat, active building | Agree | *Pending* | Agree |
| 2 | Non-urgent feedback only at natural pauses | Agree; **forecast results first** in the queue | *Pending* | Agree; queue order is a cheap config |
| 3 | Only blocking: first life-threatening warnings, and phase debriefs (deferrable) | Agree on warnings; **debrief deferral limited**: re-offered at each natural pause and next session's recap; teacher overview shows unsent; never blocks progress | *Pending* (expected difference) | See options I-B/I-C |
| 4 | At most one non-urgent pop-up per ~10 min | Agree for **pop-ups only**; log lines get their own cap (~3 per minute, merged when similar) | *Pending* (expected difference) | Agree with the ID's scope; both caps become config values |
| 5 | No new critical failure in the last ~5 min of a class | Agree and **extend**: no new forecast and no report pop-up in that window | *Pending* | Agree; needs a teacher "wrap-up" signal (Technical Lead cost: small) |
| 6 | Every prompt deferrable except life-threatening ones | Agree; a deferred forecast **expires** when its outcome is visible; logged unanswered, never wrong | *Pending* | Agree; already in QA invariant I-9 |
| 7 | — | Forecasts issued ≥ ~1 sol before their outcome | *Pending* | Agree; affects storm and radiation scheduling (Lead Game Designer) |
| 8 | — | Escalating hints only at natural pauses | *Pending* | Agree |
| 9 | — | Nothing important only in a pop-up; everything archived | *Pending* | Agree; matches story bible rule 3 |
| 10 | — | Log every interruption, deferral, expiry | *Pending* | Agree; it is how the Stage 3 interruption pass is measured |
| 11 | — | One player's prompt never pauses the group | *Pending* | Agree |

The Instructional Designer names rules 3 and 4 as the likely points of difference and says it would accept "can live with it" on a stricter pop-up limit if log lines keep their own rate cap.

### 2.3 Common ground
- Rules 1, 2, 6 (core text), 9, 11: no recorded disagreement.
- Nothing ever blocks progress except first life-threatening warnings.
- All feedback stays diegetic (colony log, Monitor, journal) and archived.
- The rules must be checked in the Stage 3 playtest with action-log counts.

### 2.4 Options

| Option | What | Instructional value | Appeal (flow immersion) | Cost |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **I-A** | Adopt TEAM.md 8.6 as drafted | Weaker: reports can be deferred forever (D1, D2, B2, B5 measures go missing); log lines throttled with pop-ups | Strongest flow | Lowest |
| **I-B** | Adopt LEARNING.md §4.6 in full (rules 1–11) | Strongest | Some risk: repeated report reminders and a busy log | Small (queue, caps, wrap-up signal, logging) |
| **I-C** (recommended) | Adopt I-B **as the Stage 3 test configuration**, with two flow guards: (a) an unsent report is re-offered **at most once per session** plus the "Since last shift" recap line, not at every pause; (b) both caps (pop-ups ~1 per 10 min, log lines ~3 per min) are config values. Set final numbers from Stage 3 action-log counts | Keeps every measure; deferral is limited, not open-ended | Bounded nagging; numbers tuned with evidence | Same as I-B |

**Recommendation: I-C.** It takes the Instructional Designer's measurement safeguards, bounds the reminders that most threaten flow, and turns the disputed numbers into config values that the Stage 3 playtest settles. That follows "evidence beats argument" (TEAM.md §4).

### 2.5 Assumptions (challenge these)
- Once per session is enough for students to send reports (unknown; the teacher overview flag is the backstop).
- A log-line cap of ~3 per minute doesn't hide important cause lines (to check in Stage 3).
- The Technical Lead can add a wrap-up signal cheaply (the session module already owns pause and resume).

### 2.6 What would make it better defined
- Stage 3 action-log counts: interruptions, deferrals, expiries, unsent reports per player.
- Session S1/S2 notes on any moment the tester felt interrupted.
