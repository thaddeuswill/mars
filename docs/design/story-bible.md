# Story Bible — Mars Terraforming

**Owner:** Lead Game Designer · **Status:** Draft 3 (2026-11-26): Round 2 fixes (classroom periods 6/14/21, "without meaning to", Keeper's Record wording), character-load check (§4.4), Landing checklist, Shift Briefings, worked example, Training Hall lobby (§3.6). Final in-game text lives in [messages/](messages/README.md) · **Depends on:** [DESIGN.md](../../DESIGN.md), [LEARNING.md](../../LEARNING.md)

This is the reference for everything the story says and how the world says it. Writers of journal entries, colony log lines, failure reports, debriefs, and the ending work from this file.

**Fiction flags.** Anything marked **[FICTION]** is invented for the game and must be labeled as invented wherever the journal touches it (LEARNING.md, "Science accuracy notes"). Anything marked **[verify]** is a real-science claim the Lead Subject Matter Expert must check before it ships.

## 1. Purpose of the Story

The story has one job: **give players a reason to care about the planet's numbers.** The Planetary Index is the lesson. The story makes the lesson personal.

| The story must… | Because… | Goals served |
|---|---|---|
| Give a clear reason to survive and build | Many classroom players are there because the teacher said so. A person to come home to, and people waiting on you, beats "make the number go up." | Engagement; C1 |
| Make every learning moment feel like part of the job | The Colony Record is framed as the **Keeper's Record**: the handover the colonists will read. What you learn is what you'll teach them. | All (A1–D2) |
| Put real moral weight on the fauna, without a lecture | D1 asks what's at stake for life already there. The story gives that life a face, a history, and fair voices on both sides. The player decides. | D1, B5 |
| Keep pointing back at Earth | Each phase has an Earth voice that compares what you just did on Mars to what is happening on Earth. | D2, A3, A5, B2 |
| Explain why things happen, through people | Failure reports and crew notes turn "it broke" into "here's where to look." | C2, B1 |
| Be honest about science | Fiction is flagged. The story never claims Mars has life or that terraforming is this fast. | Accuracy; A1–A5 |
| Feel hopeful | Ages 12–18. Isolation and loss of control are real themes here, but the story ends with a signal sent and people coming. | Engagement, wellbeing |

### Story rules (non-negotiable)
1. **The story never gates progress.** Gates are world-state thresholds (DESIGN.md §2). Story beats are *triggered by* gates and events; reading them is never *required to pass* one.
2. **No linear NPC questlines.** No one gives orders with checkboxes. Characters reach the player only through world-delivered media (section 5).
3. **Every story beat is skippable and replayable** from the journal's message archive. No time-pressured reading (LEARNING.md §0.1).
4. **No villains.** The crew made hard calls; some were wrong. The fauna are animals, not monsters. The planet is not angry; it responds.
5. **No crew deaths, no gore.** Stakes come from risk, responsibility, and the fauna, not from tragedy.
6. **Hints, not answers.** Story voices may point at a cause. They never give the full fix (LEARNING.md §4.5).
7. **No "bad ending."** The ending describes the planet the player made. It does not grade them morally.

## 2. Essential Elements

| Element | For this game |
|---|---|
| **Setting** | Cairn Dome **[FICTION]**, a small terraforming pilot dome on Mars, in the mid-northern lowlands near buried ground ice. Thin CO2 air (0.6 kPa), −60 °C, toxic perchlorate soil, dust storms, radiation **[verify: matches Index start values]**. The whole planet is the stage; the dome is home base. |
| **Protagonist** | **The Keeper** (approved): you. A systems technician sent to keep the pilot dome running during the "watch" between crews. No fixed name, gender, or face; characters call you "Keeper," never your username. In a group (3–5 players on one planet, or a small server), every player is a Keeper on the same watch team. |
| **Characters** | Three crew members who just left (via recorded messages and traces), one scientist on Earth (via messages), the station system BELL (via the colony log), and the dormant fauna (via their behavior). Only BELL and **one crew voice per phase** are essential (Hana, then Basu, then Moreno); Mensah speaks only in replies to Keeper's Reports. Section 4.4. |
| **Plot** | A storm hits right after the crew leaves. You keep the dome alive, restore contact, then push the planet toward living air. Warming wakes life the crew found but didn't tell you about. You decide how to share the planet with it, reach equilibrium, take off your helmet, and light the beacon that calls the colony ships. |
| **Conflict** | Three layers, one per phase: **Keeper vs. the planet** (survive), **progress vs. life** (the fauna, and the crew's secret), **holding a balance** (equilibrium is a band, not a finish line). |
| **Resolution** | The beacon is lit. The signal says: *the air holds, come.* The ending shows the world as you left it, fauna included, and asks what the colonists should know. |
| **Voice** | Short, plain, warm, honest. Humans sound like people under pressure who like each other. BELL sounds like a careful instrument. Section 8. |
| **Intended emotions** | Phase 1: alone, tense, then capable. Phase 2: wonder, then unease and a sense of responsibility. Phase 3: care, patience, and earned relief. Ending: pride and a quiet question. |

## 3. Backstory

### 3.1 The mission
- **The Keeper program [FICTION].** Earth's space agencies are testing whether a small team with powerful machines can start warming and thickening Mars's air at one site, so a colony can follow. The machines in the game are far stronger and faster than any real technology. The journal says so (see 3.5).
- **First Shift [FICTION].** A crew of three built Cairn Dome and its first machines over a long stay: Commander Dalia Moreno, biologist Dr. Teo Basu, and engineer Hana Kowalczyk.
- **The watch** (backstory approved). Between crews, one Keeper (or a Keeper team of 3–5 on a group planet) lives in the dome and keeps the machines running. Most of the work is automated. The Keeper is there because machines break.
- **The beacon [FICTION].** The colony fleet waits on Earth. It only launches when the site's beacon, a full-power transmitter and landing guide, sends proof that the air holds. That beacon recipe unlocks at the Equilibrium gate.

### 3.2 What happened (timeline)

| When | Event | Real-science anchor |
|---|---|---|
| Years before | Uncrewed cargo landers drop supply caches near the site. | Pre-placing cargo before crews arrive is a real mission-planning idea **[verify]**. |
| First Shift, late in their stay | Basu drills deep ice cores and finds tiny, dormant animals in a pocket of frozen brine, along with traces of the microbes he thinks they descend from. He reports to Earth. | Buried ground ice exists in Mars's mid-latitudes **[verify]**. Scientists do study whether microbes could survive in deep brines. Life on Mars: **none has ever been found. The organisms are [FICTION].** |
| Same week | Earth tells the crew: keep the samples frozen, keep the pilot running, say nothing until results are confirmed. Moreno agrees. Basu objects, on the record (the "Fridge B" argument, delivered in Phase 2). | Rules to avoid contaminating other worlds and Earth are real (COSPAR's planetary protection policy). They do **not** require keeping finds secret: "say nothing yet" was Earth's own choice, and the story must read that way (SME round 2). |
| Handover, 9 days | The Keeper arrives. Hana trains you on the machines. Nobody mentions the samples. Basu leaves a sealed note in lab Fridge B "to open when it thaws." | — |
| Launch | First Shift launches inside the departure window to meet their return ship in orbit. | Earth–Mars launch windows open about every 26 months **[verify]**. |
| 2 days later | A regional dust storm builds. Solar power drops. A seal fails, the main dish is knocked out, machines wear down. BELL seals the core module and you ride out the storm in the shielded shelter. | Mars dust storms can grow to cover the planet and have ended solar-powered rover missions **[verify: 2018 storm]**. |
| **Game start** | The storm clears. Power is low, the dome is damaged, the dish is down, and you are alone. | — |

### 3.3 Why the player is alone
**You aren't lost. You're on watch.** Being alone was always the plan. The storm made the plan dangerous. This framing matters:
- It keeps the player in control (a job to do, not a rescue to wait for).
- It makes the crew's later messages warm, not guilty-about-abandonment.
- It keeps the premise clearly different from stranded-astronaut stories. Nobody left you by accident or thought you were dead.

### 3.4 What the dormant fauna are [FICTION]
All of this is invented. The journal must say so each time it describes them.

| Topic | Story answer | Real-science hook for the journal |
|---|---|---|
| What they are | A group of small animals the crew nicknamed **thawlings** (Basu's field label: "Group S," for *sleepers*). Basu's theory: they descend from microbes that lived deep underground long ago. That ancestry is the invented part (DESIGN.md option B). | Real: scientists search for signs of past or present microbial life on Mars, and some Earth microbes (extremophiles) live in ice, brine, and deep rock **[verify]**. Some Earth animals, like tardigrades, survive freezing and drying in a dormant state called cryptobiosis **[verify]**. |
| Where they were | Dormant in frozen brine pockets deep in the ground ice, for a very long time. | Salty water stays liquid at lower temperatures than fresh water **[verify]**. |
| Why they wake | Warmth and liquid water. **Thaw gate:** the first ones stir. **Hydrosphere gate:** they reach a larger, more active **later life stage**. | Some animals wait dormant until rain or warmth returns, then wake and grow, like spadefoot toads that dig out after rain, or mosquito eggs that hatch when flooded (SME round 2 wording; not opened). |
| Why they go after machines | Emitters and extractors are the warmest, wettest, most vibrating things on the surface. The fauna are drawn to them for warmth and nesting. They are not hunting you, and only fight back if attacked. | Matches DESIGN.md: fauna pressure is the sum of machine signatures. Shrouds and quiet zones lower it; fighting doesn't. |
| What they become in Phase 3 | As biomes stabilize, they settle into them and live alongside the seeded flora. Whether they thrive depends on what the Keeper left them. | Earth link: ecosystems adjust when conditions change (HS-LS2-6) **[verify mapping]**. |

**Wording rule (decided): "later life stage," never "mutation."** The Hydrosphere fauna are a *later life stage* in every document, journal entry, and line of dialogue. "The heat made them mutate" teaches a common misconception (that individuals change their genes in response to their environment). DESIGN.md now uses the same wording.

### 3.4a Real side effects alongside the fiction
The fauna are not the only consequence of warming. Two real effects carry B5 without fiction (DESIGN.md, Hazard Systems):
- **Perchlorate.** Mars soil holds toxic perchlorate salts **[verify]**. Hana's notes warn about dust in the airlock (Phase 1) and salty meltwater (Phase 2). Basu's soil notes explain why seed kits need treated soil (Phase 3).
- **Ground collapse.** Melting ground ice under warm machines makes the ground sink. Mensah's Earth link: thawing permafrost damaging roads and buildings in the Arctic today **[verify]**.

### 3.5 Fiction notes the journal must carry
| Fiction | Honest note (one line, grade 6–7) |
|---|---|
| Speed of terraforming | "Warming Mars might take about 100 years. Filling its air with oxygen from plants could take up to 100,000 years. The game speeds it up." |
| Machine power | "No real machine can do this yet. A small test device on a rover did make oxygen from Mars air." **[verify: MOXIE]** |
| The fauna | "No life has ever been found on Mars. Scientists are still looking for tiny microbes that might live deep underground. The thawlings are made up for this game." |
| The air you can breathe | "Your lungs care how hard oxygen pushes, not just the percent. That's why the air must be thick *and* oxygen-rich." |
| Filler gas | "Earth's air is mostly nitrogen. Mars has very little. The game doesn't track it; real terraformers would have to find some." |
| Where the gas comes from | "Real Mars soil and ice don't hold enough gas to thicken the air this much. The game exaggerates it." |
| The atmosphere staying put | "Without a magnetic shield, the Sun's wind slowly strips Mars's air. That takes millions of years or more, so the game ignores it." |
| The Keeper program, dome, fleet | No note needed beyond the game's opening card: "This story is fiction. The science is as real as we could make it." |

### 3.6 Before the watch: the Training Hall (class lobby)
On a class server (several group planets, ARCHITECTURE.md §6.2), players who aren't yet assigned to a planet wait in a lobby. **Fiction: the Keeper Program Training Hall on Earth, months before the watch.** It sits *before* the story's timeline, so it can't contradict the backstory (the Keeper arrives during the handover and is already in the dome when play starts).

| Element | Spec |
|---|---|
| Place | A small indoor hall on Earth: a window with a blue sky and green hills, benches, a Mars globe, a wall chart "Earth vs. Mars" (pressure 101 kPa vs. 0.6 kPa; −60 °C vs. about 15 °C) **[verify Earth mean temperature wording with SME]** |
| Signs (all optional to read) | "Watch teams are assigned by your instructor." / "Have a team code? Use the terminal." / "Your watch starts when your team is called." |
| Join | A terminal block runs `/mars join <code>` (ARCHITECTURE.md §6.2). Assignment by the teacher works the same way |
| Safety | No damage, no hunger, no hazards, no building, no inventory carried to Mars (cleared on assignment) |
| On assignment | Screen fades. Opening card: *"This story is fiction. The science is as real as we could make it."* Then: *"Months later. Cairn Dome, Mars. The storm has cleared."* The player wakes in the shielded shelter and Landing (§5.2) begins |
| Group fiction | Each team's planet is "Cairn Dome" for that team. Signs say "Watch team 3 → Cairn Dome", never that teams share one Mars |
| Learning job | None required. The wall chart is a light A1/D2 anchor for students who wait; no message, forecast, or log event fires here |
| Standard play | No lobby (`planets.count = 1`): the opening card plays and the player starts in the shelter |

Why Earth and not an orbital deck: an orbital "transfer room" would put the Keeper in space just before landing, which contradicts the handover backstory (§3.2). A training hall needs no new fiction and gives the only Earth scene in the game, which supports the "keep pointing back at Earth" job (§1).

## 4. Characters and Relationships

There are **no NPCs to talk to**. Every character reaches the player through a medium in the world.

### 4.1 Cast

| Character | Who | Where they are | How they reach you | Voice | Learning job |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **The Keeper** (player) | Systems tech on watch | The dome | Their own journal, Keeper's Record, debrief reports | The player's own words in reflections | All |
| **Cmdr. Dalia Moreno** [FICTION] | First Shift leader | Return ship, heading to Earth | Recorded messages (backlog at Dome stable, then at gates) | Steady, direct, admits mistakes | Stakes, trust, D1 (the case *for* continuing) |
| **Dr. Teo Basu** [FICTION] | Biologist; found the organisms | Return ship | Recorded messages; field notes and sealed caches at drill sites; Fridge B note | Curious, careful, a little restless | D1 (the case for *caution*), A2, B5 |
| **Hana Kowalczyk** [FICTION] | Engineer; built the dome | Return ship | Notes taped to machines; tips attached to first-time failure reports; a few messages | Practical, joking, fond of her machines | C1, C2, B1 |
| **Dr. Sefa Mensah** [FICTION] | Climate scientist at Earth mission control | Earth | Replies to the Keeper's phase debriefs ("Earth desk" messages) | Kind teacher, curious about your data | **D2**, A3, A5, B2 |
| **BELL** [FICTION] | The dome's system: *Base Environment and Life-support Logic* | Everywhere in the dome | Colony log, failure reports, prediction prompts, Monitor | Calm, exact, never guesses. Not a personality, not a friend, never sinister. | A1, B1, B3, C2, C3 |
| **The thawlings** [FICTION] | Dormant fauna | Under the ice, then near your machines, then in your biomes | Behavior, sound, nests, tracks, Basu's notes about them | They never speak. What they do is their voice. | **D1**, B2, B5 |

**Message delay is part of the voice.** Messages take minutes to cross between planets (about 3 to 22 minutes one way, depending on where the planets are **[verify]**). Nobody can talk to you live. Every message answers something you did a while ago. This is why the crew can advise but never direct.

### 4.3 Traces left by earlier crew (world storytelling)
- Hana's notes taped to machines: "Kick gently. It likes that." / "Filters every 2 sols or it whines."
- A tally wall in the hab counting First Shift's sols.
- A dead tomato plant in the galley, with a note: "Next shift: you try."
- Basu's drill sites outside the dome, each with a marker and a sealed sample cache (Phase 2 outposts).
- Lab Fridge B: locked until the Thaw gate, then opens with Basu's note.
- Supply caches from cargo landers, labeled by Earth-side packers with small jokes.

### 4.4 Character load check (one crew voice per phase)
Rule (DESIGN.md, Context and narrative): don't overwhelm players with backstory. **Only BELL and one crew voice are essential per phase**; other characters are optional depth (archive, notes, "read more"). Mensah is the **Earth desk**: she speaks only in replies to Keeper's Reports, so she is tied to the debrief, not to the running story. She carries D2, a Primary goal (LEARNING.md §1), so her replies stay essential.

| Phase | Essential voices | Lead crew voice and why | Optional voices (archive, traces, read more) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | BELL, **Hana** | Hana built the machines the player must learn; her notes are the worked example and first-failure tips (C1, C2, B1) | Moreno's and Basu's backlog messages at Dome stable; Basu's Fridge B sticky note is a world trace, not a message |
| 2 | BELL, **Basu**, Mensah (Report 1 reply) | Basu found the thawlings; his Thaw note and his telling of the secret carry D1, A2, B5 | Moreno's own apology and Mensah's "Earth's part" (archive); the Fridge B argument (beat 11); Hana's meltwater and Armstrong-line notes |
| 3 | BELL, **Moreno**, Mensah (Report 2 and 3 replies) | Moreno hands the mission over: helmet-off line, "Your call now" (B4, D1) | Basu's "room for both" (beat 16); Hana's last tip (beat 14) |
| Ending | All, one line each | Resolution: everyone answers the beacon | — |

**Result of the check (design walkthrough, not a playtest):**
- *Before:* the essential beats used 5 named voices in Phase 1 (BELL, Hana, Moreno, Basu, Mensah) and 4 in Phase 2. A Lens A player (12, new to modpacks) would meet three crew names in one message burst at Dome stable, the same moment the first Outgasser arrives.
- *After:* 2 essential voices in Phase 1, 3 in Phases 2 and 3 (counting Mensah's debrief replies). Each crew member leads exactly one phase, so the player meets them one at a time.
- *Changes made:* beat 6 essential content is Hana's message only (§5.2); beat 10's essential message is Basu's (§5.3); Moreno's and Mensah's admissions move to the archive. Nothing is deleted: the optional lines are all still in the archive.
- *Cost:* Moreno's Phase 2 apology was a strong "adults admit mistakes" moment. Basu's version keeps it ("I said nothing too"), and Moreno's own line is one tap away.

## 5. Plot Arc and Delivery

### 5.1 The arc across the three phases

| | Phase 1: Survival Desperation | Phase 2: Industrial Geo-Engineering | Phase 3: Biome Seeding & Equilibrium |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Act** | Setup | Rising conflict | Resolution |
| **Gates** | Start → Dome stable → Phase 2 begins | Thaw → Hydrosphere → Phase 3 begins | Breathable → Equilibrium → Beacon |
| **Player wants** | To stay alive | To build and expand; then to protect what they built | To hold the balance; to finish the job well |
| **Player learns** | I can keep this place alive | My progress has side effects, and others knew | Stable is harder than fast |
| **World** | Tan sky, silent, storms, alone | Pink sky, fog, ice turning to vapor then water, life stirring | Storms fade, water pools, green spreads, air becomes breathable |
| **People** | Only BELL, then the crew's backlog arrives | Crew and Earth messages; the secret comes out | Crew hands over; Earth waits for your signal |
| **Fauna** | Unknown (a locked fridge) | Wake, then grow, then attack machines | Settle into biomes, or dwindle |
| **Core question** | Can I survive? | What did I just wake up? | What kind of world am I handing over? |
| **Emotion** | Alone → capable | Wonder → unease → responsibility | Care → relief → pride |

### 5.2 Phase 1: Setup (Survival Desperation)
**Start: Landing (controls first).** The storm has cleared. BELL's first log line reports damage and opens the **first-shift checklist**: eight short tasks inside the dome that teach movement, the suit's O2, the Monitor, sealing a block, and the airlock (DESIGN.md, Landing). Hana's notes are on every machine. The dish is dead. The only voice is BELL. Experienced players can skip the checklist with one choice ("I know the drill"); BELL then gives a 3-line summary. No forecast comes before the checklist is done or skipped. Then BELL's **Shift Briefing 1** says what this shift is about. Text: [messages/phase-1.md](messages/phase-1.md).

**Middle.** The first failures teach the dome (failure reports with Hana's tips attached the first time). The first dust storm tests the player's planning (C1). A solar event forecast introduces radiation (proposed A4 prediction prompt). Ice carried near a heater melts and boils away at once, leaving no water: in air this thin, water boils just above freezing (first A2 moment, forecast F4a; DESIGN.md ice-to-water rule, boiling-point version).

**Dome stable gate → the backlog arrives.** With life support steady, BELL can spare power for the relay link. Seven days of queued messages arrive. **Essential:** Hana's message, which hands over the first **Regolith Outgasser** plans ("Life support's steady. Time to start on the sky.") and her worked-example notes for building it (DESIGN.md, Worked example). **Optional, in the archive:** Moreno ("We saw the storm on your telemetry. We couldn't do anything. You did.") and Basu ("Keep Fridge B cold. I'll explain later. Sorry."). The mystery doesn't depend on the archive: when the relay comes up, BELL logs that Fridge B's lock is set to "Hold until thaw (T. Basu)", and Basu's sticky note on the fridge says "Keep cold. I'll explain. —T.B." **The player is no longer alone, and now has a mystery** and their first lever on the planet at the same moment (character load: §4.4).

**Phase 2 begins (pressure ≥ 5 kPa).** The sky shifts. The player sends their first Keeper's Report (Phase 1 debrief). Mensah replies from Earth: what a thicker atmosphere does on Earth (D2, A1).

### 5.3 Phase 2: Rising conflict (Industrial Geo-Engineering)
**Start.** BELL's **Shift Briefing 2**: the planet starts to answer back. Expansion. Outposts reach Basu's old drill sites. His markers and field notes hint at "something in the cores" without saying what.

**Thaw gate.** Ice near warm extractors melts into small puddles by day that freeze again at night. Warmth alone isn't enough; the water lasts only where it is warm *and* the air is thick (A2 misconception moment, building on the Phase 1 boil-away moment). Fridge B unlocks. Basu's note: "If you're reading this, it's warm enough. They might wake up. Watch them before you decide anything." Within a sol, BELL logs "unidentified motion near Extractor 1." The first thawlings appear.

**The secret.** Delivered about one sol after the first fauna sighting (gives the player time to see them first). **Essential (Basu):** "We knew they were there. Earth asked us to wait for test results. Dalia agreed. I argued, then I said nothing too. You should have heard it from us." **Optional, in the archive:** Moreno's own message ("I agreed. I should have told you.") and Mensah owning Earth's part of the decision. The recorded "Fridge B" argument between Moreno and Basu, both sides, fairly, is also in the archive (beat 11).

**Hydrosphere gate.** Surface water. The thawlings reach their **later life stage** and start damaging emitters and extractors (failure reports name fauna as the cause). The more machines the player runs, the more fauna come (B2: reinforcing loop). Conflict peaks here: the thing that moves the planet forward is the thing drawing the animals in. Basu's field notes now describe the non-lethal options (5.5). Ground near warm machines starts to sink, and Hana warns about salty meltwater (3.4a).

**Phase 3 begins (pressure ≥ 30 kPa, temperature ≥ 0 °C).** Keeper's Report 2 (Phase 2 debrief) carries the key D1 reflection: *"Was waking them worth it?"* Mensah's reply links to Earth: evidence that Earth has warmed over the past century, mainly because of human activities, as a side effect rather than a goal; and a real reinforcing loop, thawing permafrost releasing stored carbon **[verify]** (spec: prompts-and-reflections.md §3). It does **not** mention organisms revived from frozen ground: pairing a real Earth organism with the thawlings without a clear "this part is real" label breaks the Earth-link rule (fiction stays on the Mars side).

### 5.4 Phase 3: Resolution (Biome Seeding & Equilibrium)
**Start.** BELL's **Shift Briefing 3**: hold the balance. Storms weaken. The first seeded flora takes root. Moreno, now the lead crew voice, sends a short "it's your planet more than mine now" note. Optional: Hana's last tip ("Plants don't have filters. Lucky you.") and Mensah on the carbon cycle (A5, D2), folded into her Report 2 reply in classroom mode.

**Middle.** The balancing act: too much O2 and fires break out (A5, B4). Failure reports name overshoot as the cause. The thawlings change behavior near stable biomes: they graze and nest there instead of at machines. Basu: "Maybe the planet has room for both of you. I'm asking, not telling."

**Breathable gate.** The emotional peak. The player can take off the helmet. The world's sound changes (wind, not suit hiss). Moreno: "Breathe for all of us." Journal: air is breathable when it is thick enough *and* oxygen-rich enough (O2 partial pressure, at middle school depth "how hard the oxygen pushes"), with little CO2, and it took the whole system, not one machine.

**Equilibrium gate.** BELL logs the 14-sol hold as confirmed. The beacon recipe unlocks. Moreno hands over: "Your call now."

**The beacon (ending).** See 6.5.

### 5.5 The fauna choice (decided)
D1 only works if the player has a real choice about the thawlings. The user approved **non-lethal options** alongside defense (DESIGN.md, "The fauna: decision"):
- **Defend:** allowed, but it never lowers the pull of the machines, so raids keep coming.
- **Insulation shrouds:** machines run cooler and quieter (less output, fewer raids).
- **Lure stations:** draw the thawlings away, at a water cost.
- **Quiet zones:** land left without machines, where the thawlings calm down and later settle.

How the story carries it: Basu's notes describe each option as something he *would try*, never as the right answer. Moreno's messages make the case for the mission's schedule. No voice tells the player which to pick. The ending cards (6.5) show the result without judging it.

## 6. Delivery

### 6.1 Media and what each one is for

| Medium | Speaker | Length budget | Used for |
|---|---|---|---|
| **The world** | The planet, the fauna | — | Sky, fog, ice, water, storms, flora, tracks, nests. The main storyteller. Every beat has a world signal first. |
| **Colony log** | BELL | ≤ 20 words per line | Events, gates, warnings, "since last shift" recap at session start |

### 6.3 Journal
- Every gate entry follows: **what you just saw → the real science → fiction note if needed → "On Earth" line (D2) → "read more."**
- Grade band picks depth (LEARNING.md §0.1). Middle school: main text only, one Earth line. High school: "read more" adds numbers, the full Moreno–Basu argument, and a second Earth example.
- The message archive lives in the journal. Every message can be replayed; nothing is lost if skipped.

### 6.4 Keeper's Record, failure reports, debriefs
- **Keeper's Record (Colony Record):** same mechanics as LEARNING.md §4.3. Story frame only: the header reads *"What the colonists will need to know"*. Filling a goal is writing a page for them.
- **Failure reports:** BELL's voice. First time a failure type occurs, Hana's taped tip is attached. After the same cause is fixed (C2 criterion), her tips for that failure stop.
- **Phase debriefs = Keeper's Reports.** The player "sends" the debrief to Earth. The reflection answer is part of the report. Mensah's reply arrives a short time later (story delay; tune so it lands in the same session in classroom mode).

### 6.5 The ending
1. **Build and light the beacon.** The sky over the beacon brightens; BELL: "Signal sent. Delay to Earth: 14 minutes." **[delay value set from game state; verify range]**
2. **The wait.** A short silence the player can walk around in, helmet off.
3. **Replies.** Moreno, Basu, Hana, Mensah: one line each. Mensah's line is the final D2 link: what Earth can learn from a planet that had to be built by hand.
4. **Epilogue cards** (decided) describe the planet as left, read from world state. **No ranking, no score, no "good" or "bad" label.** Thresholds are in the balance sheet §6. Examples:
   - Thawlings living in biomes and quiet zones → *"The thawlings nest by the north lake. The colonists will meet them on day one."*
   - Thawlings driven off but surviving → *"The thawlings keep to the deep ice now. Basu wants to visit."*
   - Few or no thawlings left → *"Basu's samples in Fridge B are all that's left of Group S. He is studying them."*
   - Ground and water → *"Two outposts stand on sunken ground. The colonists will build on rock."* (if collapse events happened)
   - O2 margin, biome count, sols held: shown as numbers.
   - In a group, the cards describe the shared planet. One extra card per Keeper names their colony post(s) ("Life Support, then Survey"), never a rank among players.
5. **Final reflection (D1/D2):** *"The ships are coming. What should the colonists know before they land?"* Written answer goes into the Keeper's Record and the teacher export.
6. **The Keeper's Record** opens, complete, as the handover document.
7. **The game continues.** The helmet-off check stays live (DESIGN.md §1). BELL keeps logging.

### 6.6 Group play (3–5 Keepers on one planet)
- All players are Keepers on the same watch team. Messages address "Keepers" or "the Cairn team," never usernames.
- Gate beats fire once per planet and reach every online player; offline or absent players find them waiting in the archive, and BELL's "Since last shift" recap opens their next session.
- Journal, forecasts, Keeper's Record, and Keeper's Reports stay per player (LEARNING.md §0.1). Mensah's reply quotes nothing personal; it responds to the planet's data.
- **Colony posts** (DESIGN.md, Group Play): Hana's handover notes are sorted by post (Life Support, Atmosphere, Survey, Biology, Data), so each Keeper finds "their" notes. Posts rotate each phase; the story never treats one post as the lead.
- The fauna choice is a group choice. Players may disagree, as Moreno and Basu did. The ending cards describe what the group did together.
- A class runs several planets. Each planet tells the same story; the endings differ because the worlds differ. That gives the class something to compare in the final debrief.

## 7. Classroom Mode (~11–12 hours)

### 7.1 Why it holds up
- **Beats are tied to gates, not clock time.** Classroom mode reaches gates sooner because machines work faster (rate multiplier 4.0) and holds are shorter (1 / 2 / 3 sols). **No threshold is ever multiplied:** 5 kPa, 30 kPa, 0 °C, and the breathable condition are the same in every mode (DESIGN.md, Classroom Pacing). Gate numbers stay the same, so story and journal numbers stay true. Beats arrive sooner in the same order. Nothing is skipped.
- **Any time-based delay is in sols and scales.** Beat 10 (one sol after first fauna) and Mensah's replies must land in the same class period as their trigger.
- **The three debriefs match the unit plan.** The unit has 23 class periods with **17 play periods**; the class debriefs are **periods 6, 14, and 21** (LEARNING.md §5.2). Keeper's Reports 1–3 fire at gates, so a group's report may arrive before or after its class debrief; both work. Teachers can use Mensah's replies as discussion openers.
- **Session recap.** When the teacher resumes a paused world, BELL's colony log opens with *"Since last shift:"* and 2–3 lines. This covers students who missed a class.

### 7.2 Essential vs. optional
- **Essential (13 story beats plus Landing, the worked example, and 3 Shift Briefings):** 0, 1, SB1, 3, 4, 5, 6 (Hana only), 6a, 7, SB2, 9, 10 (Basu only), 12, 13, SB3, 15, 17, 19. Landing and the worked example are skippable. Across 17 play periods of ~40 minutes (LEARNING.md §5.2), the 13 story beats are a little under one per period, so most periods end with something new from the story.
- **Optional (6 beats, plus the archived crew messages from 6 and 10):** 2, 7a, 8, 11, 14, 16. In classroom mode they stay in the world as traces and archive items but don't push a notification. Beat 11 shrinks to one line inside beat 10. Beat 14 folds into Mensah's reply in beat 13. Beat 18 merges into 19.
- **Teacher "Set phase."** Essential beats from skipped phases are placed in the archive as an "Unread from earlier" bundle, marked so the teacher can assign them.

### 7.3 Text budget in classroom mode
About 15 minutes of total reading for essential story across the unit, outside journal science. Planning rate: **100 words per minute** (the Lead Instructional Designer's conservative design assumption, prompts-and-reflections.md §5; not a research norm). Count from the final text in [messages/](messages/README.md): see the word totals there. Budget check: essential story messages, Landing, Shift Briefings, and the worked example must stay under 1,500 words (15 minutes at 100 wpm).

## 8. Tone and Voice Guide

### 8.1 Overall tone
- Hopeful, honest, a little funny. Serious about risk; never scary for its own sake.
- Grade 6–7 reading level for main text. Short sentences. One idea per sentence. Common words; define a science word the first time it appears.
- The fauna are never "monsters," "evil," or "infestation." Use: *animals, thawlings, Group S, they*.
- No lecturing. Characters may disagree. The game never tells the player which side is right.
- Use **sol** for a Mars day in BELL's log; the journal explains it once.

### 8.2 Voices

| Voice | Do | Don't |
|---|---|---|
| BELL | Numbers, causes, short lines. "Likely cause." | Guess, joke, comfort, or give the fix |
| Moreno | Short, steady, owns her choices | Give orders, over-apologize |
| Basu | Questions, wonder, "I'm asking, not telling" | Preach or call anyone wrong |
| Hana | Practical tips, light jokes about machines | Give full solutions |
| Mensah | Compare to Earth, praise the player's data | Lecture or quiz |

### 8.3 Sample lines

**Journal entry (Thaw gate, middle school band):**
> The ice by the extractor melted into a puddle today. Tonight it froze again. Back when the air was thinner, ice by the heater boiled away at once. To keep water liquid, we need warmth *and* thick air. One is not enough.
> *On Earth, thick air keeps water from boiling away. That's why puddles last.*

**Colony log (BELL, Thaw gate):**
> Sol 41. Temperature −20 °C. Meltwater near Extractor 1, refroze overnight. Unidentified motion near Extractor 1. Logging.

**Failure report (BELL, with Hana's tip):**
> FAILURE: Air loss in Hab B. Likely cause: a seal cracked during the cold night. Hint: which blocks touch the outside?
> *Hana's note: "Cold finds the corners first."*

**Recorded message (Basu, after first fauna):**
> They were sleeping, Keeper. Not dead. Sleeping. And we turned up the heat. I'm not saying stop. I'm saying look at them first. Then decide.

**Recorded message (Mensah, reply to Keeper's Report 2):**
> On Earth, we are adding greenhouse gas too. You are doing it with a plan. We did it without meaning to, mostly by burning coal, oil, and gas. Your report asked if waking them was worth it. People here ask a question like it about our own frozen ground.
