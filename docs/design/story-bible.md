# Story Bible — Mars Terraforming

**Owner:** Lead Game Designer · **Status:** Draft 1, for team review · **Depends on:** [DESIGN.md](../../DESIGN.md), [LEARNING.md](../../LEARNING.md)

This is the reference for everything the story says and how the world says it. Writers of journal entries, colony log lines, failure reports, debriefs, and the ending work from this file.

**Fiction flags.** Anything marked **[FICTION]** is invented for the game and must be labeled as invented wherever the journal touches it (LEARNING.md, "Science accuracy notes"). Anything marked **[verify]** is a real-science claim the Lead Subject Matter Expert must check before it ships.

---

## 1. Purpose of the Story

The story has one job: **give players a reason to care about the planet's numbers.** The Planetary Index is the lesson. The story makes the lesson personal.

| The story must… | Because… | Goals served |
|---|---|---|
| Give a clear reason to survive and build | Many classroom players are there because the teacher said so. A person to come home to, and people waiting on you, beats "make the number go up." | Engagement; C1 |
| Make every learning moment feel like part of the job | The Colony Record is framed as the **Keeper's Record**: the handover the colonists will read. What you learn is what you'll teach them. | All (A1–D2) |
| Put real moral weight on the fauna, without a lecture | D1 asks what's at stake for life already there. The story gives that life a face, a history, and fair voices on both sides. The player decides. | D1, B5 |
| Keep pointing back at Earth | Each phase has an Earth voice that compares what you just did on Mars to what is happening on Earth. | D2, A3, A5, B2 |
| Explain why things happen, through people | Failure reports and crew notes turn "it broke" into "here's where to look." | C2, B1 |
| Be honest about science | Fiction is flagged. The story never claims Mars has life or that terraforming is this fast. | Accuracy; A1–A5 |
| Feel hopeful | Ages 12–18. Isolation and loss of control are real themes here, but the story ends with a signal sent and people coming. | Engagement, wellbeing |

### Story rules (non-negotiable)
1. **The story never gates progress.** Gates are world-state thresholds (DESIGN.md §2). Story beats are *triggered by* gates and events; reading them is never *required to pass* one.
2. **No linear NPC questlines.** No one gives orders with checkboxes. Characters reach the player only through world-delivered media (section 5).
3. **Every story beat is skippable and replayable** from the journal's message archive. No time-pressured reading (LEARNING.md §0.1).
4. **No villains.** The crew made hard calls; some were wrong. The fauna are animals, not monsters. The planet is not angry; it responds.
5. **No crew deaths, no gore.** Stakes come from risk, responsibility, and the fauna, not from tragedy.
6. **Hints, not answers.** Story voices may point at a cause. They never give the full fix (LEARNING.md §4.5).
7. **No "bad ending."** The ending describes the planet the player made. It does not grade them morally.

## 2. Essential Elements

| Element | For this game |
|---|---|
| **Setting** | Cairn Dome **[FICTION]**, a small terraforming pilot dome on Mars, in the mid-northern lowlands near buried ground ice. Thin CO2 air (0.6 kPa), −60 °C, toxic perchlorate soil, dust storms, radiation **[verify: matches Index start values]**. The whole planet is the stage; the dome is home base. |
| **Protagonist** | **The Keeper**: you. A systems technician sent to keep the pilot dome running during the "watch" between crews. No fixed name, gender, or face; characters call you "Keeper." On servers, every player is a Keeper on the same team. |
| **Characters** | Three crew members who just left (via recorded messages and traces), one scientist on Earth (via messages), the station system BELL (via the colony log), and the dormant fauna (via their behavior). Section 4. |
| **Plot** | A storm hits right after the crew leaves. You keep the dome alive, restore contact, then push the planet toward living air. Warming wakes life the crew found but didn't tell you about. You decide how to share the planet with it, reach equilibrium, take off your helmet, and light the beacon that calls the colony ships. |
| **Conflict** | Three layers, one per phase: **Keeper vs. the planet** (survive), **progress vs. life** (the fauna, and the crew's secret), **holding a balance** (equilibrium is a band, not a finish line). |
| **Resolution** | The beacon is lit. The signal says: *the air holds, come.* The ending shows the world as you left it, fauna included, and asks what the colonists should know. |
| **Voice** | Short, plain, warm, honest. Humans sound like people under pressure who like each other. BELL sounds like a careful instrument. Section 8. |
| **Intended emotions** | Phase 1: alone, tense, then capable. Phase 2: wonder, then unease and a sense of responsibility. Phase 3: care, patience, and earned relief. Ending: pride and a quiet question. |

## 3. Backstory

### 3.1 The mission
- **The Keeper program [FICTION].** Earth's space agencies are testing whether a small team with powerful machines can start warming and thickening Mars's air at one site, so a colony can follow. The machines in the game are far stronger and faster than any real technology. The journal says so (see 3.5).
- **First Shift [FICTION].** A crew of three built Cairn Dome and its first machines over a long stay: Commander Dalia Moreno, biologist Dr. Teo Basu, and engineer Hana Kowalczyk.
- **The watch.** Between crews, one Keeper (or a small Keeper team on servers) lives in the dome and keeps the machines running. Most of the work is automated. The Keeper is there because machines break.
- **The beacon [FICTION].** The colony fleet waits on Earth. It only launches when the site's beacon, a full-power transmitter and landing guide, sends proof that the air holds. That beacon recipe unlocks at the Equilibrium gate.

### 3.2 What happened (timeline)

| When | Event | Real-science anchor |
|---|---|---|
| Years before | Uncrewed cargo landers drop supply caches near the site. | Pre-placing cargo before crews arrive is a real mission-planning idea **[verify]**. |
| First Shift, late in their stay | Basu drills deep ice cores and finds tiny, dormant organisms in a pocket of frozen brine. He reports to Earth. | Buried ground ice exists in Mars's mid-latitudes **[verify]**. Life on Mars: **none has ever been found. The organisms are [FICTION].** |
| Same week | Earth tells the crew: keep the samples frozen, keep the pilot running, say nothing until results are confirmed. Moreno agrees. Basu objects, on the record (the "Fridge B" argument, delivered in Phase 2). | Rules to avoid contaminating other worlds are real ("planetary protection") **[verify: COSPAR policy]**. |
| Handover, 9 days | The Keeper arrives. Hana trains you on the machines. Nobody mentions the samples. Basu leaves a sealed note in lab Fridge B "to open when it thaws." | — |
| Launch | First Shift launches inside the departure window to meet their return ship in orbit. | Earth–Mars launch windows open about every 26 months **[verify]**. |
| 2 days later | A regional dust storm builds. Solar power drops. A seal fails, the main dish is knocked out, machines wear down. BELL seals the core module and you ride out the storm in the shielded shelter. | Mars dust storms can grow to cover the planet and have ended solar-powered rover missions **[verify: 2018 storm]**. |
| **Game start** | The storm clears. Power is low, the dome is damaged, the dish is down, and you are alone. | — |

### 3.3 Why the player is alone
**You aren't lost. You're on watch.** Being alone was always the plan. The storm made the plan dangerous. This framing matters:
- It keeps the player in control (a job to do, not a rescue to wait for).
- It makes the crew's later messages warm, not guilty-about-abandonment.
- It keeps the premise clearly different from stranded-astronaut stories. Nobody left you by accident or thought you were dead.

### 3.4 What the dormant fauna are [FICTION]
All of this is invented. The journal must say so each time it describes them.

| Topic | Story answer | Real-science hook for the journal |
|---|---|---|
| What they are | A group of small animals the crew nicknamed **thawlings** (Basu's field label: "Group S," for *sleepers*). | Some Earth animals, like tardigrades, survive freezing and drying in a dormant state called cryptobiosis **[verify]**. |
| Where they were | Dormant in frozen brine pockets deep in the ground ice, for a very long time. | Salty water stays liquid at lower temperatures than fresh water **[verify]**. |
| Why they wake | Warmth and liquid water. **Thaw gate:** the first ones stir. **Hydrosphere gate:** they reach a larger, more active life stage. | Many Earth animals have life stages triggered by temperature and water (e.g. insects, frogs) **[verify]**. |
| Why they go after machines | Emitters and extractors are the warmest, wettest, most vibrating things on the surface. The fauna are drawn to them for warmth and nesting. They are not hunting you. | Matches DESIGN.md: fauna pressure scales with active machines. |
| What they become in Phase 3 | As biomes stabilize, they settle into them and live alongside the seeded flora. Whether they thrive depends on what the Keeper left them. | Earth link: ecosystems adjust when conditions change (HS-LS2-6) **[verify mapping]**. |

**Wording rule: "life stages," not "mutations."** DESIGN.md calls the Hydrosphere fauna "mutated variants." In story text, these are a *later life stage*, not a mutation. "The heat made them mutate" teaches a common misconception (that individuals mutate in response to their environment). See Decisions needed and Handoffs.

### 3.5 Fiction notes the journal must carry
| Fiction | Honest note (one line, grade 6–7) |
|---|---|
| Speed of terraforming | "Real terraforming would take hundreds or thousands of years. The game speeds it up." |
| Machine power | "No real machine can do this yet. A small test device on a rover did make oxygen from Mars air." **[verify: MOXIE]** |
| The fauna | "No life has ever been found on Mars. The thawlings are made up for this game." |
| The atmosphere staying put | "Without a magnetic field, the solar wind would slowly strip this air away. The game ignores that." |
| The Keeper program, dome, fleet | No note needed beyond the game's opening card: "This story is fiction. The science is as real as we could make it." |

## 4. Characters and Relationships

There are **no NPCs to talk to**. Every character reaches the player through a medium in the world.

### 4.1 Cast

| Character | Who | Where they are | How they reach you | Voice | Learning job |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **The Keeper** (player) | Systems tech on watch | The dome | Their own journal, Keeper's Record, debrief reports | The player's own words in reflections | All |
| **Cmdr. Dalia Moreno** [FICTION] | First Shift leader | Return ship, heading to Earth | Recorded messages (backlog at Dome stable, then at gates) | Steady, direct, admits mistakes | Stakes, trust, D1 (the case *for* continuing) |
| **Dr. Teo Basu** [FICTION] | Biologist; found the organisms | Return ship | Recorded messages; field notes and sealed caches at drill sites; Fridge B note | Curious, careful, a little restless | D1 (the case for *caution*), A2, B5 |
| **Hana Kowalczyk** [FICTION] | Engineer; built the dome | Return ship | Notes taped to machines; tips attached to first-time failure reports; a few messages | Practical, joking, fond of her machines | C1, C2, B1 |
| **Dr. Sefa Mensah** [FICTION] | Climate scientist at Earth mission control | Earth | Replies to the Keeper's phase debriefs ("Earth desk" messages) | Kind teacher, curious about your data | **D2**, A3, A5, B2 |
| **BELL** [FICTION] | The dome's system: *Base Environment and Life-support Logic* | Everywhere in the dome | Colony log, failure reports, prediction prompts, Monitor | Calm, exact, never guesses. Not a personality, not a friend, never sinister. | A1, B1, B3, C2, C3 |
| **The thawlings** [FICTION] | Dormant fauna | Under the ice, then near your machines, then in your biomes | Behavior, sound, nests, tracks, Basu's notes about them | They never speak. What they do is their voice. | **D1**, B2, B5 |

**Message delay is part of the voice.** Messages take minutes to cross between planets (about 3 to 22 minutes one way, depending on where the planets are **[verify]**). Nobody can talk to you live. Every message answers something you did a while ago. This is why the crew can advise but never direct.

### 4.2 Relationships

| Between | Relationship | How it changes |
|---|---|---|
| Moreno → Keeper | Responsible for you; chose not to tell you about the samples | Phase 1: reassuring. Phase 2: admits the secret, apologizes. Phase 3: hands the mission to you ("Your call now. You know this planet better than I do."). |
| Basu → Keeper | Wants you to see what he saw | Phase 1: a vague teaser. Phase 2: his notes become your field guide. Phase 3: asks you, not orders you, to leave the thawlings room. |
| Hana → Keeper | Mentor; trained you for 9 days | Steady throughout. Her notes fade out as you stop needing them (fewer tips after repeat failures are fixed). |
| Mensah → Keeper | Reads your reports; treats you as a colleague | Grows warmer as your debriefs come in. Her replies always end with an Earth link. |
| BELL → Keeper | A tool that logs and warns | Does not change. Its steadiness is the point: the data is always honest. |
| Moreno ↔ Basu | Respect, and one real disagreement: continue the pilot, or pause it for the organisms | Their recorded argument (Phase 2) gives both sides fairly. Neither is the villain. The player's choices are the answer. |
| Moreno ↔ Mensah | Chain of command: Earth made the "say nothing yet" call; Moreno carried it out | Mensah owns Earth's part of the decision in Phase 2. Adults admit mistakes. |
| Hana ↔ Basu | Friendly teasing ("your freezer full of nothing") | Shows the crew as people, not mission roles. |
| Keeper ↔ thawlings | Unknown → threat → neighbors (or not) | Depends on the player. Section 5.4. |

### 4.3 Traces left by earlier crew (world storytelling)
- Hana's notes taped to machines: "Kick gently. It likes that." / "Filters every 2 sols or it whines."
- A tally wall in the hab counting First Shift's sols.
- A dead tomato plant in the galley, with a note: "Next shift: you try."
- Basu's drill sites outside the dome, each with a marker and a sealed sample cache (Phase 2 outposts).
- Lab Fridge B: locked until the Thaw gate, then opens with Basu's note.
- Supply caches from cargo landers, labeled by Earth-side packers with small jokes.

## 5. Plot Arc and Delivery

### 5.1 The arc across the three phases

| | Phase 1: Survival Desperation | Phase 2: Industrial Geo-Engineering | Phase 3: Biome Seeding & Equilibrium |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Act** | Setup | Rising conflict | Resolution |
| **Gates** | Start → Dome stable → Phase 2 begins | Thaw → Hydrosphere → Phase 3 begins | Breathable → Equilibrium → Beacon |
| **Player wants** | To stay alive | To build and expand; then to protect what they built | To hold the balance; to finish the job well |
| **Player learns** | I can keep this place alive | My progress has side effects, and others knew | Stable is harder than fast |
| **World** | Tan sky, silent, storms, alone | Pink sky, fog, ice turning to vapor then water, life stirring | Storms fade, water pools, green spreads, air becomes breathable |
| **People** | Only BELL, then the crew's backlog arrives | Crew and Earth messages; the secret comes out | Crew hands over; Earth waits for your signal |
| **Fauna** | Unknown (a locked fridge) | Wake, then grow, then attack machines | Settle into biomes, or dwindle |
| **Core question** | Can I survive? | What did I just wake up? | What kind of world am I handing over? |
| **Emotion** | Alone → capable | Wonder → unease → responsibility | Care → relief → pride |

### 5.2 Phase 1: Setup (Survival Desperation)
**Start.** The storm has cleared. BELL's first log line reports damage. Hana's notes are on every machine. The dish is dead. The only voice is BELL.

**Middle.** The first failures teach the dome (failure reports with Hana's tips attached the first time). The first dust storm tests the player's planning (C1). A solar event forecast introduces radiation (proposed A4 prediction prompt).

**Dome stable gate → the backlog arrives.** With life support steady, BELL can spare power for the relay link. Seven days of queued messages arrive at once: Moreno ("We saw the storm on your telemetry. We couldn't do anything. You did."), Hana ("Told you the filters whine."), and a short, odd one from Basu ("Keep Fridge B cold. I'll explain later. Sorry."). **The player is no longer alone, and now has a mystery.**

**Phase 2 begins (pressure ≥ 5 kPa).** The sky shifts. The player sends their first Keeper's Report (Phase 1 debrief). Mensah replies from Earth: what a thicker atmosphere does on Earth (D2, A1).

### 5.3 Phase 2: Rising conflict (Industrial Geo-Engineering)
**Start.** Expansion. Outposts reach Basu's old drill sites. His markers and field notes hint at "something in the cores" without saying what.

**Thaw gate.** Ice near extractors shrinks but leaves no puddles: at low pressure, ice turns straight to vapor (A2 misconception moment). Fridge B unlocks. Basu's note: "If you're reading this, it's warm enough. They might wake up. Watch them before you decide anything." Within a sol, BELL logs "unidentified motion near Extractor 1." The first thawlings appear.

**The secret.** Delivered about one sol after the first fauna sighting (gives the player time to see them first). Moreno: "We knew. Earth asked us to wait for test results. I agreed. I should have told you." Then Mensah owns Earth's part. In the colony log archive, the player can find the recorded "Fridge B" argument between Moreno and Basu: both sides, fairly.

**Hydrosphere gate.** Surface water. The thawlings reach their larger life stage and start damaging emitters and extractors (failure reports name fauna as the cause). The more machines the player runs, the more fauna come (B2: reinforcing loop). Conflict peaks here: the thing that moves the planet forward is the thing drawing the animals in.

**Phase 3 begins (pressure ≥ 30 kPa, temperature ≥ 0 °C).** Keeper's Report 2 (Phase 2 debrief) carries the key D1 reflection: *"Was waking them worth it?"* Mensah's reply links to Earth: thawing permafrost releasing stored carbon, and ancient life revived from frozen ground **[verify both]**.

### 5.4 Phase 3: Resolution (Biome Seeding & Equilibrium)
**Start.** Storms weaken. The first seeded flora takes root. Hana sends her last tip ("Plants don't have filters. Lucky you."). Mensah on the carbon cycle (A5, D2).

**Middle.** The balancing act: too much O2 and fires break out (A5, B4). Failure reports name overshoot as the cause. The thawlings change behavior near stable biomes: they graze and nest there instead of at machines. Basu: "Maybe the planet has room for both of you. I'm asking, not telling."

**Breathable gate.** The emotional peak. The player can take off the helmet. The world's sound changes (wind, not suit hiss). Moreno: "Breathe for all of us." Journal: what makes air breathable, and that it took the whole system, not one machine.

**Equilibrium gate.** BELL logs the 14-sol hold as confirmed. The beacon recipe unlocks. Moreno hands over: "Your call now."

**The beacon (ending).** See 6.5.

### 5.5 What the fauna story asks of design
D1 only works if the player has a real choice about the thawlings. If the only option is to kill them, the story's question is hollow. The story assumes **non-lethal options** exist alongside defense, for example deterrents that drive them away from machines, and **quiet zones** (areas with no machines nearby) where fauna stay calm. ROADMAP Stage 4 already lists "infrastructure defense options." This is a design proposal, not a settled mechanic: see Decisions needed.

**Density (standard mode).** 20 beats over ~40 h: about one major beat every 2 hours, plus traces. By phase: P1 7 beats / 8 h, P2 6 beats / 18 h, P3 6 beats / 14 h (plus the post-ending beat). ### 6.3 Journal
- Every gate entry follows: **what you just saw → the real science → fiction note if needed → "On Earth" line (D2) → "read more."**
- Grade band picks depth (LEARNING.md §0.1). Middle school: main text only, one Earth line. High school: "read more" adds numbers, the full Moreno–Basu argument, and a second Earth example.
- The message archive lives in the journal. Every message can be replayed; nothing is lost if skipped.

### 6.4 Keeper's Record, failure reports, debriefs
- **Keeper's Record (Colony Record):** same mechanics as LEARNING.md §4.3. Story frame only: the header reads *"What the colonists will need to know"*. Filling a goal is writing a page for them.
- **Failure reports:** BELL's voice. First time a failure type occurs, Hana's taped tip is attached. After the same cause is fixed (C2 criterion), her tips for that failure stop.
- **Phase debriefs = Keeper's Reports.** The player "sends" the debrief to Earth. The reflection answer is part of the report. Mensah's reply arrives a short time later (story delay; tune so it lands in the same session in classroom mode).

### 6.5 The ending
1. **Build and light the beacon.** The sky over the beacon brightens; BELL: "Signal sent. Delay to Earth: 14 minutes." **[delay value set from game state; verify range]**
2. **The wait.** A short silence the player can walk around in, helmet off.
3. **Replies.** Moreno, Basu, Hana, Mensah: one line each. Mensah's line is the final D2 link: what Earth can learn from a planet that had to be built by hand.
4. **Epilogue cards** describe the planet as left, read from world state. No ranking. Examples:
   - Thawlings living in biomes and quiet zones → *"The thawlings nest by the north lake. The colonists will meet them on day one."*
   - Thawlings driven off but surviving → *"The thawlings keep to the deep ice now. Basu wants to visit."*
   - Few or no thawlings left → *"Basu's samples in Fridge B are all that's left of Group S. He is studying them."*
   - O2 margin, biome count, sols held: shown as numbers.
5. **Final reflection (D1/D2):** *"The ships are coming. What should the colonists know before they land?"* Written answer goes into the Keeper's Record and the teacher export.
6. **The Keeper's Record** opens, complete, as the handover document.
7. **The game continues.** The helmet-off check stays live (DESIGN.md §1). BELL keeps logging.

### 6.6 Multiplayer
- All players are Keepers. Messages address "Keepers" or "the Cairn team."
- Gate beats fire once per world and reach every online player; offline players find them waiting in the archive.
- Journal, Keeper's Record, and Keeper's Reports stay per player (LEARNING.md §0.1). Mensah's reply quotes nothing personal; it responds to the world's data.

## 7. Classroom Mode (~11–12 hours)

### 7.1 Why it holds up
- **Beats are tied to gates, not clock time.** Classroom mode reaches gates sooner (threshold multiplier ~0.3), so beats arrive sooner in the same order. Nothing is skipped.
- **Any time-based delay is in sols and scales.** Beat 10 (one sol after first fauna) and Mensah's replies must land in the same class period as their trigger.
- **The three debriefs match the unit plan.** Keeper's Reports 1–3 line up with class periods 5, 12, and 18 (LEARNING.md §5.2). Teachers can use Mensah's replies as discussion openers.
- **Session recap.** When the teacher resumes a paused world, BELL's colony log opens with *"Since last shift:"* and 2–3 lines. This covers students who missed a class.

### 7.2 Essential vs. optional
- **Essential (13 beats, 12 without beat 5):** 1, 3, 4, 5 (if adopted), 6, 7, 9, 10, 12, 13, 15, 17, 19. Across ~16 play periods of ~40 minutes (LEARNING.md §5.1), that is a little under one essential beat per period, so most periods end with something new from the story.
- **Optional (5 beats):** 2, 8, 11, 14, 16. In classroom mode they stay in the world as traces and archive items but don't push a notification. Beat 11 shrinks to one line inside beat 10. Beat 14 folds into Mensah's reply in beat 13. Beat 18 merges into 19.
- **Teacher "Set phase."** Essential beats from skipped phases are placed in the archive as an "Unread from earlier" bundle, marked so the teacher can assign them.

### 7.3 Text budget in classroom mode
About 15 minutes of total reading for essential story across the unit, outside journal science. Sample check: 13 beats × ~60 words ≈ 780 words of story messages, about 4–6 minutes at a typical middle school reading pace **[verify reading-rate assumption with ID]**.

## 8. Tone and Voice Guide

### 8.1 Overall tone
- Hopeful, honest, a little funny. Serious about risk; never scary for its own sake.
- Grade 6–7 reading level for main text. Short sentences. One idea per sentence. Common words; define a science word the first time it appears.
- The fauna are never "monsters," "evil," or "infestation." Use: *animals, thawlings, Group S, they*.
- No lecturing. Characters may disagree. The game never tells the player which side is right.
- Use **sol** for a Mars day in BELL's log; the journal explains it once.

### 8.2 Voices

| Voice | Do | Don't |
|---|---|---|
| BELL | Numbers, causes, short lines. "Likely cause." | Guess, joke, comfort, or give the fix |
| Moreno | Short, steady, owns her choices | Give orders, over-apologize |
| Basu | Questions, wonder, "I'm asking, not telling" | Preach or call anyone wrong |
| Hana | Practical tips, light jokes about machines | Give full solutions |
| Mensah | Compare to Earth, praise the player's data | Lecture or quiz |

### 8.3 Sample lines

**Journal entry (Thaw gate, middle school band):**
> The ice by the extractor is shrinking, but there are no puddles. The air here is so thin that ice skips being water. It turns straight into gas. To keep water liquid, we need more warmth *and* more air pressure. One is not enough.
> *On Earth: our thick air is why puddles last.*

**Colony log (BELL, Thaw gate):**
> Sol 41. Temperature −20 °C. Ice loss near Extractor 1. Unidentified motion near Extractor 1. Logging.

**Recorded message (Basu, after first fauna):**
> They were sleeping, Keeper. Not dead. Sleeping. And we turned up the heat. I'm not saying stop. I'm saying look at them first. Then decide.
